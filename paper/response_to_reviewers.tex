\concern{Editor, Concern \#1}

\concern{Editor comment:}

\rvcomment{``Please address the label-dependent aggregation rule by reporting primary results under a label-independent rule and providing sensitivity analyses under alternative tie policies. Also discuss the limitations of case construct validity and statistical independence assumptions.''}

\rvcomment{-Shiping Wang}

\textbf{Author response:}

\begin{itemize}

\item We agree, and the correction is larger than a change of rule. Decision~D4 as preregistered is already label-independent, and the published analysis deviated from it.

\item The decisions log (\texttt{decisions\_log.md}, D4, 2026-07-15) specifies the per-case decision as the modal replicate decision with ``ties broken toward flag''. The original preregistration repeats this, and the confirmatory preregistration for this paper states ``modal flag over replicates (ties$\rightarrow$flag)'' and then defines a false positive on benign cases by that same rule. No amendment alters it.

\item The implemented benign branch required a strict majority, so a tie was not counted as a false positive. That is a deviation from D4 and it was not disclosed. The suspicious branch followed D4 correctly.

\item The label-independent rule requested here is therefore the preregistered rule, and we have restored it rather than adopted a new one.

\item Under the preregistered rule the false-positive rates are 2.3, 7.7, 37.0, 44.0 and 85.7 percent, a spread of 83.3 pp (95\% CI 71.8--93.7). The deviation understated every false-positive rate; the spread was almost unaffected, at 82.7 pp.

\item The deviation most likely arose at the pivot. D4's stated rationale is that ties toward flag keep the \emph{miss} estimate conservative, which was correct when the estimand was misses. After the estimand became false positives, ties toward flag no longer favoured the conservative direction on that axis, and conservatism appears to have been reapplied per endpoint.

\item Sensitivity: the spread is 77.7 to 85.3 pp across five aggregation rules and four single-call configurations, with identical model ordering in all nine.

\item On construct validity, no expert adjudication or inter-rater check was performed. Labels are correct by construction of the generator only.

\item On independence, the cluster bootstrap over the eight typology strata is now applied to the primary test. A cluster-level permutation test gives p below 0.001, with 0 of 2,000 draws reaching the observed statistic.

\end{itemize}

\textbf{Author action:}

\begin{itemize}

\item Section 2.5 was rewritten to state decision~D4 as preregistered, to report the deviation in the benign branch, and to make the preregistered label-independent rule primary.

\item A deviations subsection was added recording the departure, the date it was detected, and its effect on every affected number.

\item The Appendix~A sentence attributing a label-dependent rule to preregistration was corrected; D4 does not condition on the case label.

\item A new table reports five aggregation rules and four single-call configurations on both the false-positive and miss axes.

\item Section 4.1 gained two limitation paragraphs, one on label construct validity and one on the independence assumptions of the primary tests.

\item Table 4 retains the deviating rule alongside the preregistered rule so that both sets of numbers remain in the record.

\end{itemize}

\concern{Reviewer \#3, Concern \#1}

\concern{Reviewer comment:}

\rvcomment{Major comment --- label-dependent aggregation rule:}

\rvcomment{The preregistered aggregation rule resolves 2--2 replicate splits differently depending on the known ground-truth label: a tie on a legitimate case is treated as legitimate, whereas a tie on a suspicious case is treated as detected. Although this rule was preregistered and transparently disclosed, it uses information that would not be available in deployment and therefore may produce an optimistically biased evaluation operating point. This is especially important because ties affected a non-trivial number of model--case decisions. I recommend reporting the primary results under a label-independent aggregation rule and providing sensitivity analyses under alternative tie policies, such as treating ties as abstentions, always escalating ties, or analysing each prompt configuration separately. The authors should also clarify whether the headline false-positive and miss rates represent deployable model configurations or an evaluation-only aggregation procedure.}

\textbf{Author response:}

\begin{itemize}

\item We accept the criticism, and investigating it uncovered more than the reviewer could have seen from the paper.

\item The rule is optimistic in exactly the way described: both tie directions favoured the model and the branch was selected by the ground-truth label.

\item It is also not the preregistered rule. Decision~D4, the original preregistration and the confirmatory preregistration for this paper all specify the modal decision with ties broken toward flag, with no reference to the case label. The benign branch as implemented required a strict majority. That is an undisclosed deviation, and we report it as such.

\item The label-independent rule the reviewer recommends is therefore the preregistered rule. We have restored it as primary, which also happens to be the conservative choice on the false-positive axis and corresponds to a real control, namely routing a split panel to a human reviewer.

\item We ran each of the three sensitivity analyses named. Ties as abstentions gives a spread of 84.9 pp on retained cases; always escalating gives 83.3 pp; each prompt configuration separately gives 85.3, 85.0, 80.3 and 81.3 pp. Unanimity gives 77.7 pp. The ordering never changes.

\item On the final question: the four-call aggregate is an evaluation-only construct. No institution would run four calls per transaction and apply a majority rule, so the published rates were not deployable configurations.

\item The deployable quantity is a single call. Across the four single-call configurations the false-positive rate runs from 0.3 to 2.3 percent for Gemini Flash and from 80.7 to 87.7 percent for GPT-4o-mini, with spreads of 80.3 to 85.3 pp.

\item The direction of the bias is worth stating plainly. The deviation understated every false-positive rate, and on the miss axis the preregistered rule raises miss rates from 12.0, 3.3, 3.7, 0.7 and 0.0 percent to 18.3, 8.3, 7.0, 2.0 and 0.0 percent. The paper's central claim, the size of the cross-model spread, is unaffected.

\end{itemize}

\begin{itemize}

\item Section 2.5 states decision~D4 as preregistered and makes it primary.

\item A deviations subsection records the departure in the benign branch, when it was detected, and every number it affected.

\item The Appendix~A sentence attributing a label-dependent rule to preregistration was corrected.

\item A new table reports five aggregation rules and four single-call configurations on both axes, covering each requested sensitivity analysis.
